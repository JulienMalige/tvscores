Privacy policy

Last updated 14 November 2026

TV Scores has no account and no ads, and unless you agree to share how you use it, it collects no personal data. Here is everything that leaves your Apple TV, iPhone or iPad, and where it goes.

What the app asks for

To show scores, the app asks the TV Scores server (tvscores.julienmalige.com) for the games, the tables and the pictures. Each request carries your device's time zone, so that games fall on the right day (the one for the games also names the sports this version of the app can show), and nothing about you: no name, no email address, no identifier.

What the server keeps

The sports data it serves, and nothing about who asks: no IP address, no device identifier, no history of what you looked at.

Usage data, if you agree

From version 1.1, the app asks you once whether you want to share how you use it, and sends nothing unless you say yes. If you do, it sends Amplitude (Amplitude, Inc., in the United States), the analytics service its developer uses: the pages you open and for how long, what you choose there (a competition, a game, a day, an app under How to Watch, a setting), when you open and leave the app, the app's version, your device's model and system version, your languages, region and time zone, your choices in Settings (the competitions you follow and their order, the countries, the apps you hid), and a random number made for this, which tells your visits apart without saying who you are. Amplitude also receives your IP address, as any server does, keeps it, and works out your approximate city from it.

On iPhone and iPad, the app also records its screens during your visits, so that they can be replayed (Amplitude's Session Replay). The Apple TV records nothing of the kind.

This is used to understand how TV Scores is used and to make it better. It is not sold, not used for advertising, and not combined with other companies' data. Amplitude handles it under its own privacy policy, and takes part in the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework. You can stop at any time in the app's Settings, under Usage Data: from then on, nothing more is sent.

Cloudflare

Requests reach the server through Cloudflare, which carries and protects the traffic. Like any network that carries web traffic, Cloudflare sees your IP address, and handles it under its own privacy policy.

Pictures

Crests, photos and app icons come from the TV Scores server. Now and then, when the server does not have a picture yet, your device loads it from the site that publishes it, such as TheSportsDB, which sees your IP address as any website would.

What stays on your device

Your choices (the competitions and their order, the countries, the apps and the language) are saved on your Apple TV, iPhone or iPad. They leave it only within the usage data above, if you agreed to share it.

Other apps

How to Watch opens another app, a broadcaster's or a streaming service's, or its page on the App Store. What happens there is up to that app and its own privacy policy. The usage data says which app you chose, never whether it opened.

Apple

If you chose, in your device's settings, to share analytics with app developers, Apple may send anonymous crash reports and usage figures for TV Scores. That is Apple's doing, under Apple's privacy policy, and you can turn it off in those settings.

Test versions

Test versions of the Apple TV app handed out through Apple's TestFlight also send the server an anonymous record of how the remote moves through the app (what took the focus, which menu opened) to find and fix navigation faults. It is tied to a random number made when the app is installed, not to you. The App Store version sends none of it, and neither does the app on iPhone or iPad.

Children

TV Scores is not made for children in particular and asks no one's age. Usage data leaves the device only with the yes of whoever uses it.

Changes

If any of this changes, this page will say so first, with a new date.
